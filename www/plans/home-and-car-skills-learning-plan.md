# Home and Car Skills Learning Plan

## Goal

Become calm and useful when something at home or in a car goes wrong. Learn the first safe checks, practise simple maintenance, and recognise when qualified help is needed.

The aim is not to repair everything yourself. A good first response is a valuable skill.

## How to learn

Use a real task with your father or grandfather as the lesson. Before they begin, ask:

- What are we trying to fix?
- What could make this unsafe?
- What can I do while you work?
- What tells us to stop and call someone?

For each lesson, write down the symptom, first checks, tools, hazards, stop conditions, and contact person. Start with observation and preparation; only take over a task when the experienced person says it is safe.

## Learning path

### 1. Know the home

- Find the water meter and household shutoff with a family member.
- Find the electrical distribution board (DB) and label its breakers.
- Keep appliance manuals, warranty details, and service contacts together.
- Ask the landlord, Town Council, or managing agent about responsibilities.
- Learn where to report common-area faults in your building.

Do not open electrical panels or test live wiring. If there is sparking, a burning smell, a hot socket, or repeated breaker trips, stop using the affected circuit and arrange qualified help.

### 2. Plumbing and water

- Learn to spot leaks, drips, slow drains, and toilet cistern leaks.
- Practise cleaning removable drain covers and hair traps.
- Learn where to shut water off, but practise only with permission.
- For a suspected leak, turn off water fittings and check the meter.
- Record photos and details before contacting the responsible party.

PUB lists certain simple plumbing jobs that handymen may perform. Regulated water-service and sanitary plumbing work requires a PUB Licensed Plumber; check the current list before arranging work. [PUB: Plumbing Works](https://www.pub.gov.sg/Professionals/Requirements/Licensed-Plumbers/Plumbing-Works)

Escalate active leaks, sewage smells or backflow, recurring blockages, hidden pipe leaks, and any water near electrical equipment. For major pipe bursts or supply interruptions, contact PUB; for a flat-specific issue, contact the appropriate owner or building manager. [PUB: Help and Feedback](https://eservices.pub.gov.sg/helpandfeedback/report)

### 3. Electricals and appliances

- Learn to replace a bulb only when the switch is off and fixture cool.
- Match the bulb type and rating to the fitting instructions.
- Practise resetting a tripped breaker only after identifying the affected appliance.
- Unplug damaged appliances; never touch exposed wires.

In Singapore, installation, repair, modification, or replacement of switches, lighting points, sockets, wiring, or circuit breakers is electrical work for an EMA Licensed Electrical Worker. [EMA: Engaging Licensed Workers](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/engaging-licensed-workers)

### 4. General household troubleshooting

Use this sequence: describe the problem, check for immediate danger, check power or water, inspect visible connections, consult the manual, try one safe reset, then record the result and escalate.

Learn small non-electrical tasks over time: tighten a loose cabinet handle, replace remote batteries, clean appliance filters, and use basic hand tools safely. Check permission and product instructions first.

### 5. Wi-Fi and home internet

- Identify the fibre termination point, modem/ONT, router, and mesh nodes.
- Learn what each status light means from the device manual.
- Check whether one device or every device is affected.
- Restart equipment in the provider's recommended order.
- Note the time, lights, error messages, and affected rooms before calling support.

Avoid factory resets unless the provider or device instructions tell you to; they can erase network settings.

### 6. Basic car care

If you have access to a car, learn from its owner and manual:

- Check tyre pressure when tyres are cold; use the vehicle placard.
- Learn the warning lights, spare-tyre location, jack points, and tools.
- Practise tyre changing only on level ground, away from traffic.
- Learn how to call roadside assistance and share your location.
- Check engine oil and washer fluid only as the manual directs.

On an expressway, do not attempt a tyre change. Switch on hazard lights, move off the carriageway if possible, keep passengers away from traffic, get behind the guardrail, and arrange recovery. [Singapore Police Force: Driving in Traffic](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Advisories/TP/FT-ENG-2126.pdf) [LTA: Driving on Expressways and in Tunnels](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/driving/road_safety_and_vehicle_rules/driving-in-expressway-and-tunnel.html)

## Progress checks

After each topic, aim to:

1. Explain the system in plain language.
2. Name two safe first checks.
3. Identify at least two stop conditions.
4. Find the correct manual, professional, or support contact.
5. Help with one supervised task without rushing.

## Suggested first month

- **Week 1:** Map the home shutoffs, DB, manuals, and contacts.
- **Week 2:** Learn leak signs and clean one removable drain cover.
- **Week 3:** Identify Wi-Fi equipment and practise fault reporting.
- **Week 4:** Review car manual, tyre pressure, and roadside plan.

Treat electrical switches and regulated plumbing as observation-and-escalation lessons, not DIY practice.
